# Choreograph explanation and expression

## 1. Define the story

Identify the audience, one product message, the primary subject, and the outcome a viewer must understand. Keep the headline, proposition, navigation, pricing, and conversion controls available while the demonstration plays. Use real product capabilities in the scene.

**Complete when:** the message, focal subject, readable endpoint, and surrounding utility controls are explicit.

## 2. Write the beat sheet

Read [timing](../references/timing.md) and the narrative/ambient entries in [patterns](../references/patterns.md). Write setup → transformation → result, with purpose, focal subject, movement, duration, overlap, and reading hold per beat. Add secondary or ambient movement only when it supports the primary subject.

For repeated elements, compute the full stagger budget. Choose viewport-triggered or scroll-linked behavior intentionally; preserve native reading/navigation control. Define replay, pause/stop where applicable, reverse/fast-scroll handling, and the equivalent static explanation.

**Complete when:** each beat contributes to the message; the sequence budget, repetition rule, and reduced-motion/static path are defined.

## 3. Deliver and judge comprehension

Use the beat-sheet contract in [outputs](../references/output-contracts.md). Prefer a prototype that can pause at meaningful states when available. Evaluate whether the viewer understands the feature and finds the next action separately from aesthetic preference. Verify surrounding content remains usable if animation setup fails.

**Complete when:** normal and static paths convey the same product truth, the outcome is readable, and claimed comprehension results are backed by evaluation or labeled as hypotheses.
